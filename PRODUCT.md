## Users

The people who buy and evaluate gravity.integration are IT staff and technical decision-makers at Polish mid-market companies — manufacturing, FMCG, automotive, and chemical firms — who need to connect specific business systems (ERP, WMS, CRM, e-commerce, couriers) that don't talk to each other today. They are not developers looking for a framework; they are practitioners with a concrete integration problem and a shortlist to build. They arrive knowing their own stack (often Comarch, Subiekt, enova, Graffiti.ERP, BaseLinker, Allegro) and want to confirm this tool handles *their* systems.

A large share of the real pipeline originates in the Graffiti.ERP ecosystem via referrals and software houses, so a secondary audience is those partners and integrators who recommend or resell the product. The site speaks primarily to the end buyer, but should never feel foreign to a Graffiti.ERP partner sending a client to it.

## Product Purpose

gravity.integration is a Polish ESB (Enterprise Service Bus) platform — currently v4 — built by Caffeine Minds sp. z o.o., part of the DM Investments group that has developed the Graffiti.ERP system for over 20 years. The site is the product's marketing surface: it explains what an ESB is and why it matters, proves the product connects the visitor's specific systems (139+ ready connectors across 13 categories), shows real Polish deployments, and states pricing openly. Success is qualified activation — free downloads first, then demo bookings and direct contact — with the strongest leverage being the Graffiti.ERP ecosystem, where being the default integration partner is the growth engine.

## Positioning

The Polish, locally-installed ESB that connects any ERP, CRM, WMS, or e-commerce system without writing code — 139+ ready connectors, installed in 15 minutes, from 2 999 PLN/rok — and more than a classic ESB, with Always-on-data, IntelliChain, and firstSIGNAL. For companies in the Graffiti.ERP world, it is the native way to make every other system talk to Graffiti.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: download the free version (`/pobieranie/`, the MailerLite flow). Secondary CTA: book a 15-minute demo (`#section-demo`, CF7 form 414). Contact and the ROI calculator are supporting paths for visitors not ready for either.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: a Polish ESB that connects ERP, CRM, and WMS in 15 minutes — 139+ connectors, from 2 999 PLN/rok.
- Belief ladder: (1) my systems not talking to each other is a real, costly problem; (2) an integration bus (ESB) is the right category of fix, not more point-to-point scripts; (3) gravity.integration connects *my* specific systems — including my ERP — out of the box; (4) it's credible: real Polish manufacturers run it, and it's backed by 20+ years of Graffiti.ERP engineering; (5) it's low-risk and affordable — free to try, 15-minute install, priced openly from 2 999 PLN/rok → download.
- Proof on hand: four case studies, all X → Graffiti.ERP (TIP-TOPOL couriers, Kapitan Navi RCP/Roger, ELMET Poznań × BaseLinker, NAC × cStore — NAC metrics still pending); a 22-logo client wall (Bispol, Frogum, Hewalex, Lindner, and others); full KRS/company registration; and the 20-year Graffiti.ERP / Caffeine Minds heritage. Marketing claims are held to what's provable — "139+" connectors, never inflated.

## Brand Personality

Confident, concrete, and Polish-engineering. The voice is direct and technical without hiding behind jargon, leads with proof rather than hype, and is unusually open about price. There is a deliberate "buntownicy / rewolucjoniści" (rebels / revolutionaries) edge in the developer- and business-facing hero copy, but it is grounded, not loud-challenger noise — the authority comes from two decades of actually building enterprise ERP software, so the tone can afford to be plain-spoken and self-assured. Polish-first throughout, with English kept ready as a first-class future locale.

## Anti-references

Not the old WordPress site it replaces: a 41-snippet, output-buffer-hacked build on a black-box agency theme, fighting Locomotive Scroll bugs and shipping ~13.7 MB of autoplay video — invisible-until-scroll content, broken sticky headers, and maintenance dread. Not a developer-tool or GitHub-community play: a dev-community positioning was explicitly evaluated and rejected, because the buyers are IT practitioners, not framework shoppers. Not generic SaaS-template design: no hero-metric template, no endless identical icon-heading-text card grids, no cream/beige "AI default" palette. And a deliberate distance from the direct competitor lsesb.com.

## Design Principles

Speak to IT buyers, not developers — frame everything as concrete integration outcomes (systems connected, data flowing, hours saved), not APIs and SDKs. Prove, don't claim — real named companies, real named systems, honest pricing, and every number defensible. Make the specific fit tangible — the visitor's own ERP/CRM/WMS should be visibly present (the 139+ directory exists largely to let a buyer find their system by name). Lower the risk at every step — Polish, locally installed, free to try, 15-minute setup; reduce perceived commitment from the hero to the download. Let performance and clarity be part of the pitch — the whole reason for the rebuild is that a fast, clean, maintainable site is itself evidence the company can build good software; no bloat, no gimmicks.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA: body text at ≥4.5:1 and large text at ≥3:1 against its background, full keyboard operability, visible focus, and correct alt text on the content and logo imagery. Motion respects `prefers-reduced-motion` (the rebuild already ships reduced-motion alternatives and no-JS fallbacks so content is never gated behind an animation). Polish is the primary language with `lang` set correctly and an English locale kept i18n-ready (`/en/` + hreflang) rather than bolted on later.
